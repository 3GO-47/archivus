# A R C H I V U S
*An oracle of all recorded history.*

**Live: https://3go-47.github.io/archivus/**

The archive ends in **The Horizon — Anticipated** (2026–2140). Anticipated events are not a separate theme: they live in their real categories (a fusion plant is Science, a sea-level projection is Climate) and wear a dashed spectral-white ring in every view — `event.future` is a flag, filterable via the Horizon era. Each carries its epistemic grade: orbital mechanics is certain (Apophis 2029, Halley 2061, Venus 2117), schedules slip (Artemis, Mars), projections carry ranges (IPCC, UN), and claims (the Singularity) are recorded as claims, filed beside the prophecies they descend from. Every future record is wired to its historical ancestors — Fusion back to the Manhattan Project, AMR back to Fleming, the urban century back to Uruk.

**Sharing:** see `SHARE.md` — any single `mobile/*.html` file can be sent directly; `exports/archivus-site.zip` is a deploy-ready static site (Netlify Drop / tiiny.host / GitHub Pages give an instant public URL).

**On a phone or tablet:** the `mobile/` folder holds a self-contained single-file build of every instrument — all data, styles and code inlined — so each file works alone in any viewer (Documents by Readdle, Files, a mail attachment). Copy the whole `mobile/` folder across and the cross-links between instruments work too. The main folder's pages require the full directory structure and are best served or opened from the folder itself.

**How to learn from it:** open the Codex and simply read — or press **T** in the Archive for the ten narrative tours — including *The Great War* and *The Second World War* (v8), *The Gods' Roads* (how pantheons travel, Lion-Man to the Florentine Codex), *The Forecast* (the four grades of tomorrow, taught by example) and *The Long Engines* (fire, plague, crowd, mind and sky followed straight through NOW and out the other side). Click any era band and step through time with ← →; consult the Oracle (**O**) on any window; every record deep-links across all six instruments. All of it is touch-ready — pinch to zoom on a phone.

The instruments cross-reference each other: event records cite the Transmissions that touch them; Transmissions and Bloodline entries deep-link back into the Chronicle; the Oracle reports the codex's claims on any time window. The Bloodline is also published as the persisted artifact **`archivus-bloodline`**.

## Run it

No build, no server, no dependencies. **Open `index.html` in any modern browser.** All data ships as plain script files so everything works from `file://`; fonts load from Google when online and degrade gracefully offline.

## The frame (v10)

Eleven pages, one instrument. `src/nav.js` is the first thing in every `<body>` and gives each page the same header — brand, the eleven instruments, search — in place before first paint, so nothing moves between pages except a gold marker that glides from the instrument you left to the one you entered. Page changes cross-fade (native cross-document view transitions on http(s) in browsers that have them, with the hovered page prerendered; a hand-made fade on `file://` and elsewhere). Each instrument reopens where you left it: view, selection and time window are remembered for the session. The first time you enter an instrument a title card passes; after that, nothing interrupts.

## Seen at a glance (v11)

v11 was built to one rule: what matters should be on screen without scrolling for it.

- **The Archive's time axis** is no longer pure log time. On that axis the 114 anticipated years of the Horizon took a third of the default view and the whole twentieth century under a twelfth. The axis is now log time *stretched where the record is thick* (`core.js`, "the time scale"): every stretch of time still gets room for its length, plus room for how much happened in it. The Horizon now takes about a tenth of the default view; 1900–2000 about a fifth. Pan, zoom, flights and the Wheel are unchanged.
- **The standing digest.** On a wide screen a reading of whatever is in view stays docked beside the chart: the eras spanned, the count, what dominates (click a theme to filter by it), the defining records in time order (click one to fly to it) and the powers active. It re-reads the window as you pan, zoom or filter. `×` folds it away; `◨ In view` brings it back. Selecting a record or asking the Oracle uses the same panel.
- **More names.** A label that has no room to the right of its node is now tried on the left before being dropped, and no label runs off the chart.
- **Long views are cut into sections.** Any reading view that ran to many screens (the War Room's Powers and Cost, the Doctrines' Manifesto, Red Family and Library) now carries a pinned row of section chips and shows one section at a time; *All* restores the single long page and is remembered. A link that jumps to something inside a section opens it first. The two new instruments are built this way from the start: every view of the Realms and the Coups fits one screen at 1440×900.
- **The portal** is a three-by-three board with doors to the two new instruments.

## Three colourways (v12)

The header carries a three-way switch at its right edge: **Blue** (the original navy observatory), **Black** (neutral greys on true black, no blue cast) and **White** (dark ink on a white page). The choice is remembered, follows you to every instrument, and can be forced in a link with `?theme=black` or `?theme=white`. On screens narrower than 1560px the switch folds into one three-coloured disc; press it to step to the next colourway.

**How it works.** The blue palette is still the only one written by hand. Every colour in the stylesheets, the data files and the scripts stays as it was; Black and White are *derived* from it by one rule in `src/theme.js`:

- A colour keeps the contrast it had against the navy page, measured against the new page. Black re-anchors the greys and drains their blue; White mirrors lightness, which preserves the contrast between any two colours, so hierarchy survives. Hue is kept (the arithmetic is done in OKLab).
- Text is never left below 4.5:1 against its panel (WCAG AA) in any colourway, the original included. Accents used as text are deepened or lifted just enough to hold that; see-through text is judged as it lands on the page.
- Accents keep their identity: gold is gold, the Allied blue is blue, a theme's colour on the Chronicle matches its legend chip.
- On White, a mark that was dim on navy (the red end of a map scale, a quiet purple) is held to 3:1 against the page, the standard for graphics, instead of mirroring to a pale wash; no further, so what was quiet does not shout. An element with nothing in it (a swatch, a bar, a dot) counts as a mark, so legends match their maps.

The rule reaches the page three ways. `tools/build-themes.js` reads the four shared stylesheets and each page's own `<style>` and writes `src/themes.css`: a zero-specificity twin of every colour-carrying declaration, pointing at a variable, plus one table of 336 variables per colourway. Colours that scripts write into the page (SVG `fill`, inline `style`) are re-toned as they arrive by a `MutationObserver`; canvas drawing (the Astrolabe, the Bloodline minimap, image export) is re-toned at the 2D-context setters. Blue runs none of the run-time layer.

**Kept as they are.** The Codex is a parchment book in every colourway (`data-fixed` on `#cx-book`; the generator skips `.cx-*`). The switch's own swatches are not re-toned.

**Checked.** A contrast audit walks 39 view states of the eleven pages in each colourway and measures every visible text element against what is actually behind it. Before this pass 371 (blue), 494 (black) and 547 (white) elements fell below AA; what remains in each is deliberate and still legible in context: the quadrant watermarks on the two compasses, and powers, leaders and labels dimmed because they are not yet active or not selected (their dimming was eased from 28–42% to 45–58% opacity). Fixes made at the source, so the original blue gained too: slate captions lifted, lane labels on the Chronicle raised from 60% to 90%, the faint inks of the Codex deepened to 4.5:1 on parchment, heat-table cells on the Coups capped so their numbers stay readable.

**To change a colour:** edit it where it is written (blue), then run `node tools/build-themes.js` and `node tools/build-mobile.js --zip`. **To change how Black or White are derived:** edit `map()` in `src/theme.js` and rebuild. **For a one-off exception:** add a rule to `src/themes-extra.css` (appended to the generated file) and say why. **For an object that must keep its own colours:** add its selector to `FIXED` in the generator and `data-fixed` to its element.

## The Realms and the Coups: where the numbers come from (v11)

`src/data/realms.js` and `src/data/coups-record.js` are **generated** — do not edit them by hand. `tools/build-realms.py` builds both from:

- **V-Dem v16** (country-year dataset, 1789–2025), as distributed in the `vdemdata` R package: `python3 tools/build-realms.py path/to/vdem.RData` (needs `pip install pandas pyreadr pycountry`). It carries Powell & Thyne's coup counts and V-Dem's historical-regimes record.
- **Freedom House, *Freedom in the World 2026*** — `research/data/freedomhouse-fiw2026.psv`, as captured from freedomhouse.org; every row is checked (political rights + civil liberties = total).

Everything derived is computed in the open in that script: the five-year drift, the colour key for claimed ideology, and the six "pressure signals" (a coup attempt or a forced ending within ten years; the liberal-democracy index down 0.10+ in five years; V-Dem's political-violence and polarisation scores above stated thresholds; a military executive). The signals are a count of recorded conditions, labelled as such everywhere they appear — not a probability and not a forecast. One V-Dem entry is set aside as a plain miscoding and listed in the script (the United States' Civil Rights Act of 1964, entered as a coup).

The hand-written layers are `realms-notes.js` (form of government for all 200; 57 short briefs), `realms-usa.js` (the United States) and `coups.js` (the roll and the two studies). Their house rule is in the file headers: **the archive does not pick a side.** Disputes are stated as each side states them; "where is it heading" is given as rival readings with evidence for and against; classing a seizure by creed and aim is declared judgment, and where calling an event a coup at all is disputed the entry says so. `node tools/validate-realms.js` checks all of it.

**To add a seizure to the roll:** append a row to `list` in `coups.js` — `[id, year, name, place, iso3, kind, creed, aim, outcome, text, wikipedia-title]`. **To add it to a study:** add `[id, road, conditions]` to `red.cases` or `green.cases`. **To update the United States:** add rows to `tl`, and revise `topics`, `bills` and each reading's `pro` / `con` / `watch` in `realms-usa.js`.

## The design

Dark observatory: obsidian, brass, parchment. Physics-driven interaction — eased flights in log-time, inertial coasting, rAF-throttled rendering. Selected events become atoms with counter-orbiting electrons; importance-9+ events emit shockwave halos; relation threads carry flowing energy; approximate dates are dashed, contested datings render as faded uncertainty bands, and the BCE/CE meridian stands as a golden spoke in every view.

## Data & schema

All data lives in `src/data/` as window-global script files (no fetch, so `file://` works):

- `events*.js` — the archive. Each event: `id, title, startYear` (negative = BCE), `endYear|null, approximate, era, region, civ, theme, importance (1–10), desc, source, related[]`. Coordinates in `geo.js` (`id → [lat, lon]`), scholarly dating ranges in `uncertainty.js` (`id → [min, max]`).
- `eras.js` / `themes.js` — 14 era bands; 10 themes + 11 regions (the glow palette).
- `transmissions.js` — the codex: category (angelic/divine/daimonic/alchemical/oneiric), transmitter, what was taught, claim source & date, the documented record (innovation, first attestation, first principles), gap, epistemic note, links.
- `lineage2.js` — the Bloodline: nodes (board designations verbatim, houses, notes), edges (solid = descent, `1`-flagged = marriage/union, `gap` = unnumbered generations), plus owner-editable `unionExtra`/`unionNot` overrides.
- `tours.js` — narrative tours: arrays of event ids + one line of narration each. Add your own.
- `wars-ww1.js` / `wars-ww2.js` — the War Room's datasets (schema documented at the top of `wars-ww1.js`): `countries` (allegiance timelines per modern country), `powers`, `phases`, `items` (battles & turning points, each with a source URL), `fronts` (keyframed lines the app interpolates), `rings` (the Japanese perimeter), `areas`, `moves` (offensives), `theaters`, `cost`, `leaders` + `ties`, `causes` / `aftermath` / `tech`, `sources`.
- `events7.js` — the bridge: every war item flagged `ar: [importance, theme, region, civ, related[]]` becomes an archive record (59 of them), so the wars' text lives in one place. Also publishes `ARCHIVUS_WAR_LINKS` (event → War Room deep link), exact `date` / `dateLabel` fields, and the two war tours.

- `doctrines.js` / `doctrines-marx.js` — the Doctrines (schema at the top of each file). One `nodes` list drives four views: an **ideology** has a family `f` and compass coordinates `x` (−10 left … +10 right), `y` (−10 libertarian … +10 authoritarian), or `xc` + `span` if it cuts across the chart; a **root** is a forerunner (`k`: t/o/e). Every node carries `yr`, `pl`, `at`, `d` (what it holds, in its own terms), `c` (its critics' case), `u` (source) and `fr: [[parentId, kind, note]]` with kind `s` source / `x` against / `p` split. `doctrines-marx.js` holds the Manifesto dissection, the Red Family matrix, the schisms, the record and the party-state table.
- `omni.js` — the search-everything index, **generated** by `tools/build-index.js` (1,598 rows: `[label, kind, href, sub, weight]`); fetched the first time the palette opens. Never edit by hand.
- `events8.js` — the second bridge: nodes flagged `ar: [importance, theme, region, civ, title]` become archive records (19 of them, plus the Soviet famine and the Great Terror). Publishes `ARCHIVUS_DOCTRINE_LINKS` and two tours.

**To add an event:** append to `events4.js` following the schema, add its coordinate to the same file's pattern (`at: [lat, lon]`), reload.

**To add a battle to the War Room:** append an item to `items` in the right `wars-*.js` file (`id, n, s, e, at, k, w, d, f, u`); add `ar: [...]` if it should also appear in the Archive. **To move a front:** add a key `{ d: "YYYY-MM-DD", p: [[lat, lon], …] }` — the app resamples and interpolates. Validate with `node tools/validate-wars.js`.

**To add a doctrine:** append a node to `doctrines.js` with its family, coordinates, origin, `d`, `c`, `u` and `fr`; it appears on the Compass, Lineage, Atlas and Library at once. Placements are editorial conventions — keep the critics' case beside every doctrine. Validate with `node tools/validate-doctrines.js` (`--urls` lists every citation).

**After any change:** `node tools/build-mobile.js --zip` regenerates the search index and rebuilds the eleven single-file `mobile/` builds and `exports/archivus-site.zip`. Sourcing standards and the research trail live in `research/`.

## Epistemics

Three registers, never blurred: **the record** (dated, sourced — Wikipedia orientation links, flagged for cross-checking in `research/sources.md`), **the claim** (the board's designations, the codex's transmissions — preserved verbatim, attributed), and **legend** (dashed, labeled — the Grail thread, the mythic strata). Contested dates carry visible uncertainty bands; interpolated chronologies say so. *The archive takes no position on daimons. It takes positions on dates.*
