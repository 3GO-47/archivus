# A R C H I V U S
*An oracle of all recorded history.*

**Live: https://3go-47.github.io/archivus/**

Each carries its epistemic grade: orbital mechanics is certain (Apophis 2029, Halley 2061, Venus 2117), schedules slip (Artemis, Mars), projections carry ranges (IPCC, UN), and claims (the Singularity) are recorded as claims, filed beside the prophecies they descend from. Every future record is wired to its historical ancestors — Fusion back to the Manhattan Project, AMR back to Fleming, the urban century back to Uruk.

**Sharing:** see `SHARE.md` — any single `mobile/*.html` file can be sent directly; `exports/archivus-site.zip` is a deploy-ready static site (Netlify Drop / tiiny.host / GitHub Pages give an instant public URL).

**On a phone or tablet:** the `mobile/` folder holds a self-contained single-file build of every instrument — all data, styles and code inlined — so each file works alone in any viewer (Documents by Readdle, Files, a mail attachment). Copy the whole `mobile/` folder across and the cross-links between instruments work too. The main folder's pages require the full directory structure and are best served or opened from the folder itself.

**How to learn from it:** open the Codex and simply read — or press **T** in the Archive for the ten narrative tours — including *The Great War* and *The Second World War* (v8), *The Gods' Roads* (how pantheons travel, Lion-Man to the Florentine Codex), *The Forecast* (the four grades of tomorrow, taught by example) and *The Long Engines* (fire, plague, crowd, mind and sky followed straight through NOW and out the other side). Click any era band and step through time with ← →; consult the Oracle (**O**) on any window; every record deep-links across all six instruments. All of it is touch-ready — pinch to zoom on a phone.

The instruments cross-reference each other: event records cite the Transmissions that touch them; Transmissions and Bloodline entries deep-link back into the Chronicle; the Oracle reports the codex's claims on any time window. The Bloodline is also published as the persisted artifact **`archivus-bloodline`**.

## Run it

No build, no server, no dependencies. **Open `index.html` in any modern browser.** All data ships as plain script files so everything works from `file://`; fonts load from Google when online and degrade gracefully offline.

## The frame (v10)

Fourteen pages, one instrument. `src/nav.js` is the first thing in every `<body>` and gives each page the same header — brand, the fourteen instruments, search — in place before first paint, so nothing moves between pages except a gold marker that glides from the instrument you left to the one you entered. Page changes cross-fade (native cross-document view transitions on http(s) in browsers that have them, with the hovered page prerendered; a hand-made fade on `file://` and elsewhere). Each instrument reopens where you left it: view, selection and time window are remembered for the session. The first time you enter an instrument a title card passes; after that, nothing interrupts.

## Seen at a glance (v11)

v11 was built to one rule: what matters should be on screen without scrolling for it.

- **The Archive's time axis** is no longer pure log time. On that axis the 114 anticipated years of the Horizon took a third of the default view and the whole twentieth century under a twelfth. The axis is now log time *stretched where the record is thick* (`core.js`, "the time scale"): every stretch of time still gets room for its length, plus room for how much happened in it. The Horizon now takes about a tenth of the default view; 1900–2000 about a fifth. Pan, zoom, flights and the Wheel are unchanged.
- **The standing digest.** On a wide screen a reading of whatever is in view stays docked beside the chart: the eras spanned, the count, what dominates (click a theme to filter by it), the defining records in time order (click one to fly to it) and the powers active. It re-reads the window as you pan, zoom or filter. `×` folds it away; `◨ In view` brings it back. Selecting a record or asking the Oracle uses the same panel.
- **More names.** A label that has no room to the right of its node is now tried on the left before being dropped, and no label runs off the chart.
- **Long views are cut into sections.** Any reading view that ran to many screens (the War Room's Powers and Cost, the Doctrines' Manifesto, Red Family and Library) now carries a pinned row of section chips and shows one section at a time; *All* restores the single long page and is remembered. A link that jumps to something inside a section opens it first. The two new instruments are built this way from the start: every view of the Realms and the Coups fits one screen at 1440×900.
- **The portal** is a three-by-three board with doors to the two new instruments.

## Three colourways (v12)

The header carries a three-way switch at its right edge: **Blue** (the original navy observatory), **Black** (neutral greys on true black, no blue cast) and **White** (dark ink on a white page). The choice is remembered, follows you to every instrument, and can be forced in a link with `?theme=black` or `?theme=white`. On screens narrower than 1620px the switch folds into one three-coloured disc; press it to step to the next colourway.

**How it works.** The blue palette is still the only one written by hand. Every colour in the stylesheets, the data files and the scripts stays as it was; Black and White are *derived* from it by one rule in `src/theme.js`:

- A colour keeps the contrast it had against the navy page, measured against the new page. Black re-anchors the greys and drains their blue; White mirrors lightness, which preserves the contrast between any two colours, so hierarchy survives. Hue is kept (the arithmetic is done in OKLab).
- Text is never left below 4.5:1 against its panel (WCAG AA) in any colourway, the original included. Accents used as text are deepened or lifted just enough to hold that; see-through text is judged as it lands on the page.
- Accents keep their identity: gold is gold, the Allied blue is blue, a theme's colour on the Chronicle matches its legend chip.
- On White, a mark that was dim on navy (the red end of a map scale, a quiet purple) is held to 3:1 against the page, the standard for graphics, instead of mirroring to a pale wash; no further, so what was quiet does not shout. An element with nothing in it (a swatch, a bar, a dot) counts as a mark, so legends match their maps.

The rule reaches the page three ways. `tools/build-themes.js` reads the four shared stylesheets and each page's own `<style>` and writes `src/themes.css`: a zero-specificity twin of every colour-carrying declaration, pointing at a variable, plus one table of 336 variables per colourway. Colours that scripts write into the page (SVG `fill`, inline `style`) are re-toned as they arrive by a `MutationObserver`; canvas drawing (the Astrolabe, the Bloodline minimap, image export) is re-toned at the 2D-context setters. Blue runs none of the run-time layer.

**Kept as they are.** The Codex is a parchment book in every colourway (`data-fixed` on `#cx-book`; the generator skips `.cx-*`). The switch's own swatches are not re-toned.

**Checked.** A contrast audit walks 39 view states of the eleven pages in each colourway and measures every visible text element against what is actually behind it. Before this pass 371 (blue), 494 (black) and 547 (white) elements fell below AA; what remains in each is deliberate and still legible in context: the quadrant watermarks on the two compasses, and powers, leaders and labels dimmed because they are not yet active or not selected (their dimming was eased from 28–42% to 45–58% opacity). Fixes made at the source, so the original blue gained too: slate captions lifted, lane labels on the Chronicle raised from 60% to 90%, the faint inks of the Codex deepened to 4.5:1 on parchment, heat-table cells on the Coups capped so their numbers stay readable.

**To change a colour:** edit it where it is written (blue), then run `node tools/build-themes.js` and `node tools/build-mobile.js --zip`. **To change how Black or White are derived:** edit `map()` in `src/theme.js` and rebuild. **For a one-off exception:** add a rule to `src/themes-extra.css` (appended to the generated file) and say why. **For an object that must keep its own colours:** add its selector to `FIXED` in the generator and `data-fixed` to its element.

## The Conflicts (v13)

`conflicts.html` — every armed conflict and armed standoff under way as of 14 November 2026: 40 of them, on one ladder from tension to major war.

| View | What it shows |
|---|---|
| **The Map** | Countries shaded by the worst conflict on their soil; a pin per conflict; a diamond per armed group, filled where a government has listed it as a terrorist organisation. Under the map, the scale itself: five columns, tension → kinetic warfare, with every conflict in its place. |
| **The Ladder** | The same 40 as cards in five columns, deadliest first within each. |
| **The Toll** | One row per conflict: deaths in 2025 and in 2026 from a single comparable table, and one figure per side, labelled by who gave it. |
| **The Belligerents** | The 89 armed groups and listed bodies, with their dates on the United States and United Kingdom lists (60 and 19; 27 are on neither). |
| **Method & sources** | How levels are set, what the figures can bear, and all 66 sources. |

**The scale.** 1 Tension (no killing between the parties) · 2 Crisis (force used, under about 100 deaths a year) · 3 Armed conflict (100–1,000) · 4 War (1,000–10,000, or open combat between armed forces) · 5 Major war (over 10,000). The thresholds are the Uppsala programme's and Wikipedia's; the five names follow the Heidelberg barometer. The level is the Archive's reading by that rule. The arrow (worsening / unchanging / easing) is taken from the Council on Foreign Relations' tracker and is left off where no outside assessment was found.

**Losses.** Each side's losses are given as stated and labelled: *own account*, *opponent's claim*, *outside estimate*, or *count* (by name). Claims are never added to counts and nothing is totalled across sources. Where a side publishes nothing, the card says so.

**Designations.** A designation is a government's act under its own law, and lists differ, so two full lists are shown side by side: the US State Department's Foreign Terrorist Organizations and the UK Home Office's proscribed organisations. The page takes no view on who belongs on either.

**Honest limits.**
- It is a snapshot. Every card carries the newest report read for it; the yearly death table stops in late August 2026.
- Eight smaller conflicts (Cameroon, Mozambique, the Philippines, India's insurgencies, West Papua, Western Sahara, Brazil, Kosovo–Serbia) rest on the comparable table and the lists alone: no dated November 2026 report was read for them, and their cards say so.
- The yearly table's rows are sometimes wider than the conflict named (the Sahel row runs from Algeria to Nigeria; Sudan's takes in South Sudan; Afghanistan's covers every conflict there). Each card says where.
- Per-side losses do not exist for most conflicts. The page does not estimate them.
- Whole-country shading is used only where fighting covers much of the country; Russia, India, Saudi Arabia, Brazil and Indonesia are not shaded for wars at their edge.
- Wikipedia is used for the comparable table and for tabulations of each party's claims, alongside primary and news sources (UN bodies, the State Department, the Home Office, CFR, ACLED, Critical Threats, Al Jazeera, WAFA, Times of Israel, Mediazona and others).

**To update it:** edit `src/data/conflicts.js` (the header explains every field), set `meta.asOf`, then `node tools/validate-conflicts.js` and `node tools/build-mobile.js --zip`. The validator refuses a figure without a source, a map name that is not on the map, or a designation without the list in the card's sources.

## A statement and what it rests on (v17)

The owner, of the Oracle: "needs to be more intuitive and do a better job with the space… too much wasted space and no easy way to get from a statement made in the oracle to its substantiation." And of the Coups: "why is there no map of the red and green seizures?"

**The Oracle's Reading is now statements beside their evidence.** The eight deductions stand in a column on the left. Choose one and everything it rests on is beside it, in place: the figures, the charts, the numbered facts with their sources, the graded deductions that follow, the case against, what to watch. The loom and the ledger follow below, unchanged. The link `oracle.html#v=reading&r=war` opens a statement directly; the search box finds statements too.

**Every deduction says what it rests on.** In any dossier (the Reading's evidence, or a line in the Lines) the facts are numbered, and each graded deduction carries a row: *Rests on · fact 3 · fact 5 · chart · Armed conflicts*. Press one and it lights and comes into view; press the deduction and everything it rests on lights; hovering a fact shows which deductions lean on it. Where a deduction rests on something that is not a figure on the page, the row says so in words, and where it rests on a dated expectation it links to the Horizon.

That mapping is data, written by hand: `window.ARCHIVUS_ORACLE.rests` at the foot of `src/data/oracle.js`, one row per deduction (`[facts, charts, note, horizon id]`). `tools/validate-oracle.js` refuses a deduction with no row, an index that does not exist, or one that rests on nothing and says nothing about it. When a line's `shows` or `deduce` is edited, its rows must be edited with it.

**Space.** The Reading's and the Horizon's mastheads are a third of their old height (the Horizon's filter sits beside its title); the Lines' list is 300px, not a third of the page; the four boxes of a dossier are two independent columns, so a short box no longer leaves a hole beside a long one. One function, `dossier()` in `src/oracle-app.js`, draws the evidence for both views.

**The seizure map (the Coups).** The Red Seizures and the Green Seizures each open on a new first section, *The map*: every country where a Marxist or an Islamist seizure in the roll took place, on one map. Solid is a seizure that held, paler one that was brief, palest one that failed; a country where both creeds seized carries the other's outline. A year control ("up to 1917 / 1949 / 1979 / 1991 / today") shows each family spreading. A third layer, off until asked for, marks governments that claim a socialist or communist, or a religious, ideology today (V-Dem, through the Realms; `tools/build-claims.js` writes the two short lists to `src/data/coups-claims.js`). Click a country for its cases.

The map is labelled as what it is: where a creed took or tried to take a state. A seizure is not an alliance, a creed that held power once may not hold it now, and V-Dem's "religious" covers every faith, not only Islam. The cases, the preconditions and the other ledger are the next three sections, as before; `#v=red&s=cases` opens the cases.

Tests in the project: `tools/tests/oracle.js` (25 checks), `tools/tests/seizures.js` (12), `tools/tests/trim.js`.

## The trim (v16, reworked in v16.2)

The owner asked for the whole of Archivus to be made less cluttered — **reorganised, not cut**. v16.0 got that wrong in three places (it took the Wheel out of the Archive's switch, hid seven tabs behind a small switch under new names, and hid the War Room's blurb); v16.2 put all of it back in a reorganised place. **Nothing is hidden or removed.** The rule for any later change: a thing may be grouped, made smaller, or folded behind a control that names it — never taken out of reach.

All of it is written once, in `src/nav.js` and the last block of `src/styles.css` ("THE TRIM").

**1 · Five doors in the header, not thirteen links.** `GROUPS` in `src/nav.js`:

| Door | Holds |
|---|---|
| Oracle | the Oracle (alone, first: it is what the project is for) |
| Record | Archive, Astrolabe, Codex; the Trial under a rule |
| Power | Realms, Coups, Doctrines |
| War | War Room, Conflicts |
| Myth | Bloodline, Pantheons, Transmissions |

A door opens a menu of its instruments, each with one line saying what it is. **2 · Grouped tabs: every view keeps its own tab and its own name.** A view that is the same subject arranged another way stands beside the tab it belongs with, smaller and after a dot, and that tab stays lit while it is open:

## The illumination (v15)

The Oracle set a standard and the other twelve instruments did not meet it. v15 is one shared layer that brings them up to it, so the look is defined once and not copied page by page.

Where it lives:

- **`src/styles.css`, last block ("THE ILLUMINATION")** — the styles. Every selector carries `body.has-rail`, so it outranks each page's own sheet without `!important`.
- **`src/nav.js`, `illuminate()`** — the two things CSS cannot do alone. It runs on every page except the Oracle, which draws its own.

What it does:

1. **A masthead at the head of every view.** The page's sign in a ringed emblem, a line such as "The Realms · IV of V", the view's name in engraved capitals, and what the view is for (taken from the tab's own tooltip). It is inserted at the top of each scrolling `.pane`, or at the head of a view that has none; map views keep the whole stage. The Transmissions and the Trial, which have no tabs, get one for the page. Views are re-rendered by their engines, so a `MutationObserver` puts the masthead back.
2. **Light under everything drawn.** One SVG filter (`#ax-bloom`: a blurred copy of the drawing laid beneath it) is defined once and given to charts, sparklines, the Doctrines' compass and lineage, the War Room's timeline and the two family trees through the class `.lux`. It is switched off in the White colourway, where it reads as a smudge.
3. **Framed panels.** Tiles, cards and columns get a lit surface, a deep shadow and two brass corners. Side panels get light falling from the top.
4. **Engraved headings.** Section headings carry a ✦; the chapter titles of the War Room and the documents of the Doctrines are set the way the Oracle sets its lines.
5. **Quiet motion.** Cards lift toward the pointer; the emblem's ring turns once every 140 seconds. All of it stops under "reduce motion".

One behaviour changed on purpose: a view with a masthead **scrolls** when its content is taller than the screen, instead of squeezing its charts into what is left (`.pane > .grid2 { flex: 1 0 auto }`, charts at least 230px tall).

To dress a new page: give it `#masthead` with an `h1` and a `.tagline`, use `.pane` inside `.wr-view` sections with `#wr-tabs` buttons that carry a `title`, and class its charts `chart`. Nothing else is needed.

Not changed: the Archive's Chronicle, the Astrolabe and the Codex keep their own drawing (they were already the richest pages); only their side panels were touched.

## The Oracle (v14)

**What the project is for.** The Archive gathers the record in one place; the Oracle (`oracle.html`, thirteenth instrument, last in the header) asks what that record points to. It is the tab for deductions: where each long line of history is heading, how far that can be trusted, and the best case against it.

Four views:

- **The Reading** — the eye, then **the loom**: all eleven lines on one frame from the dawn of farming to 2100, one row each on a shared, broken time axis, the Archive's eras along the top, the present as one bright thread through all of them and the 40 dated expectations at the foot. Under it, eight deductions as numbered tablets, and the ledger of every line (sparkline, the figure now, the verdict, the grades).
- **The Lines** — each of the eleven in full, in the order the owner cares about them: **Power** (who makes the world's goods), **Orders**, **War**, **Rule**, **Force**, **Machines**, **Life and wealth**, **People**, **Plagues**, **Energy**, and **Climate** last. *Trends* are things that have moved one way; *returns* (Rule, Force, Orders, Plagues) are things that have happened before, with a base rate. Each has an emblem, four headline figures, its charts, **what the record shows**, **what it points to** (graded), **the case against**, **what to watch**, links into the other instruments, and its sources.
- **The Horizon** — the Archive's 40 dated expectations for 2027–2117, sorted by the kind of knowledge each rests on: *Clockwork* (an orbit, the calendar), *Scheduled* (an institution's plan), *Projected* (a trend or a model), *Claimed* (a forecast by people with a stake in it).
- **Method & sources** — the five rules, the four grades, what the instrument cannot do, the Archive's own lean (computed), and every series with its underlying source.

Rules it is built on:

1. **The whole record.** Every chart starts at its source's first year: population and towns from 10,000 BCE, carbon dioxide from 800,000 years ago, world output from year 1, democracy and regime endings from 1789–90. Where the last 75 years would vanish against 12,000, the time axis is **broken into equal panels** and the cuts are drawn. A source that starts late (conflict counts, 1989; internet use, 2005) says so in its title.
2. **No number is typed.** The sentences in `src/data/oracle.js` carry `{{tokens}}`; `facts()` in `src/oracle-app.js` computes all 194 from the data when the page opens. Update a series and the text changes with it.
3. **Every deduction is graded** by what it rests on — *In the pipeline* (already set in motion), *Likely* (a long trend or a well-populated base rate), *Contested* (the record points both ways), *Speculative* (a proponent's forecast with nothing to test it against) — and stands beside the case against it.
4. **Arithmetic is labelled as arithmetic.** The Oracle's own extrapolations (the thirty-year temperature trend carried forward; one pandemic per 52 years since 1817) are marked as such. Other people's projections (the UN's population figures) are named as theirs.

Data:

| File | What it holds |
|---|---|
| `src/data/oracle.js` | The words: grades, the seven sentences, the ten lines, the threads (which Archive records count as polities, pandemics, steps in information technology), the Horizon classes, the method. Hand-written; header documents every field. |
| `src/data/oracle-series.js` | 38 world series, 1,470 points (seven are each power's share of world output, derived from the Maddison Project's GDP figures). **Generated** by `node tools/build-oracle.js` from `research/data/owid-world-series.json`. |
| `research/data/owid-world-series.json` | The series as read from Our World in Data on 24 Nov 2026, with each one's underlying source and update date. |
| (existing) `realms.js`, `coups-record.js`, `conflicts.js`, `events*.js` | The Archive's own record: democracy since 1789, regimes ended by force since 1790, conflicts under way, and the events from which polity lifespans, pandemic intervals and the Horizon are computed. |

`node tools/validate-oracle.js` checks that every token has a value, every record, series and link exists, every Horizon record has an https source, and that the seventeen sentences whose truth depends on the data (for example "three of the four calmest decades are this century's") still hold. It fails loudly if new data makes a sentence false.

To refresh: re-read the series into `research/data/owid-world-series.json` (same shape), run `node tools/build-oracle.js`, `node tools/validate-oracle.js`, then `node tools/build-mobile.js --zip`. To add a line: add an object to `lines` in `src/data/oracle.js` (with its accent colour `c`, a `loom` spec for its row on the loom — `line`, `multi`, `marks`, `spans` or `cols` — and three or four `stats`); a new figure needs one line in `facts()`. The order of `lines` is the order everywhere.

How it is drawn (v14.1): every line of a chart is drawn twice, a blurred copy under a crisp one (`lit()`; the blur is one SVG filter defined in `oracle.html`), with a fading area beneath and a pulsing ember at its latest point. Panels carry two brass corners (`.or-panel`). The colourway engine re-tones all of it, gradients included.

Known limits (also on the Method view): the returns computed from the Archive's own records inherit its lean (Europe is 31% of records; 40% are dated after 1800); "polities" mixes dynasties and empires and under-counts short-lived states; seven pandemics are not a sample; the world averages hide distribution; nothing here measures trust, belief or the living world. The series were thinned when stored (every point before 1800, then every fifth or tenth year).

The Archive's own toolbar already has an **Oracle** button, which summarises whatever period is in view. The two are complementary: that one reads a window, this one reads the whole.

## The United States, from 1607 (v13.1)

The United States view of the Realms first shipped with a timeline that began in 2000 and a chart that began in 1900. That was a real gap: a country's direction cannot be read from its last two years. The view now carries the whole story.

- **Standing** — the two democracy indices from **1789**, the first year V-Dem scores the country, to 2025.
- **The long arc** (new section) — twelve eras from the colonies to the present. Each gives the governing idea, who governed and who could vote, what they fought over, how it ended, the index at its start and end, and its turning points.
- **Timeline** — **117 events from Jamestown (1607) to this month**: 70 for 1607–1999, the 11 of the run-up since 2000, and the 36 of 2025–26. Ten lanes (two new: *Constitution & union*, *Rights & citizenship*); the eras run across the top; click a lane name to follow one thread through four centuries.
- Several entries are there because today's disputes turn on them: *Wong Kim Ark* (birthright citizenship), *Humphrey's Executor* (the president's power to dismiss), the Pendleton Act (the civil service), the Twenty-second Amendment, the War Powers Resolution, the Voting Rights Act's pre-clearance rule.

**Sources.** 34 of the 70 historical events cite the primary document at the US National Archives ("Milestone Documents"); the rest cite a Wikipedia article. Every slug and title was checked against the Archives' own list or a live Wikipedia link before it went in — two titles recalled from memory turned out to be wrong and were corrected. **Not covered.** Nothing before English settlement: the Native nations, and Spanish and French America, are in the Archive's own records, not on this tab.

## The Realms and the Coups: where the numbers come from (v11)

`src/data/realms.js` and `src/data/coups-record.js` are **generated** — do not edit them by hand. `tools/build-realms.py` builds both from:

- **V-Dem v16** (country-year dataset, 1789–2025), as distributed in the `vdemdata` R package: `python3 tools/build-realms.py path/to/vdem.RData` (needs `pip install pandas pyreadr pycountry`). It carries Powell & Thyne's coup counts and V-Dem's historical-regimes record.
- **Freedom House, *Freedom in the World 2026*** — `research/data/freedomhouse-fiw2026.psv`, as captured from freedomhouse.org; every row is checked (political rights + civil liberties = total).

Everything derived is computed in the open in that script: the five-year drift, the colour key for claimed ideology, and the six "pressure signals" (a coup attempt or a forced ending within ten years; the liberal-democracy index down 0.10+ in five years; V-Dem's political-violence and polarisation scores above stated thresholds; a military executive). The signals are a count of recorded conditions, labelled as such everywhere they appear — not a probability and not a forecast. One V-Dem entry is set aside as a plain miscoding and listed in the script (the United States' Civil Rights Act of 1964, entered as a coup).

The hand-written layers are `realms-notes.js` (form of government for all 200; 57 short briefs), `realms-usa.js` (the United States) and `coups.js` (the roll and the two studies). Their house rule is in the file headers: **the archive does not pick a side.** Disputes are stated as each side states them; "where is it heading" is given as rival readings with evidence for and against; classing a seizure by creed and aim is declared judgment, and where calling an event a coup at all is disputed the entry says so. `node tools/validate-realms.js` checks all of it.

**To add a seizure to the roll:** append a row to `list` in `coups.js` — `[id, year, name, place, iso3, kind, creed, aim, outcome, text, wikipedia-title]`. **To add it to a study:** add `[id, road, conditions]` to `red.cases` or `green.cases`. **To update the United States:** add rows to `tl`, and revise `topics`, `bills` and each reading's `pro` / `con` / `watch` in `realms-usa.js`.

## The design

Dark observatory: obsidian, brass, parchment. Physics-driven interaction — eased flights in log-time, inertial coasting, rAF-throttled rendering. Selected events become atoms with counter-orbiting electrons; importance-9+ events emit shockwave halos; relation threads carry flowing energy; approximate dates are dashed, contested datings render as faded uncertainty bands, and the BCE/CE meridian stands as a golden spoke in every view.

## Data & schema

All data lives in `src/data/` as window-global script files (no fetch, so `file://` works):

- `events*.js` — the archive. Each event: `id, title, startYear` (negative = BCE), `endYear|null, approximate, era, region, civ, theme, importance (1–10), desc, source, related[]`. Coordinates in `geo.js` (`id → [lat, lon]`), scholarly dating ranges in `uncertainty.js` (`id → [min, max]`).
- `eras.js` / `themes.js` — 14 era bands; 10 themes + 11 regions (the glow palette).
- `transmissions.js` — the codex: category (angelic/divine/daimonic/alchemical/oneiric), transmitter, what was taught, claim source & date, the documented record (innovation, first attestation, first principles), gap, epistemic note, links.
- `lineage2.js` — the Bloodline: nodes (board designations verbatim, houses, notes), edges (solid = descent, `1`-flagged = marriage/union, `gap` = unnumbered generations), plus owner-editable `unionExtra`/`unionNot` overrides.
- `tours.js` — narrative tours: arrays of event ids + one line of narration each. Add your own.
- `wars-ww1.js` / `wars-ww2.js` — the War Room's datasets (schema documented at the top of `wars-ww1.js`): `countries` (allegiance timelines per modern country), `powers`, `phases`, `items` (battles & turning points, each with a source URL), `fronts` (keyframed lines the app interpolates), `rings` (the Japanese perimeter), `areas`, `moves` (offensives), `theaters`, `cost`, `leaders` + `ties`, `causes` / `aftermath` / `tech`, `sources`.
- `events7.js` — the bridge: every war item flagged `ar: [importance, theme, region, civ, related[]]` becomes an archive record (59 of them), so the wars' text lives in one place. Also publishes `ARCHIVUS_WAR_LINKS` (event → War Room deep link), exact `date` / `dateLabel` fields, and the two war tours.

- `doctrines.js` / `doctrines-marx.js` — the Doctrines (schema at the top of each file). One `nodes` list drives four views: an **ideology** has a family `f` and compass coordinates `x` (−10 left … +10 right), `y` (−10 libertarian … +10 authoritarian), or `xc` + `span` if it cuts across the chart; a **root** is a forerunner (`k`: t/o/e). Every node carries `yr`, `pl`, `at`, `d` (what it holds, in its own terms), `c` (its critics' case), `u` (source) and `fr: [[parentId, kind, note]]` with kind `s` source / `x` against / `p` split. `doctrines-marx.js` holds the Manifesto dissection, the Red Family matrix, the schisms, the record and the party-state table.
- `omni.js` — the search-everything index, **generated** by `tools/build-index.js` (1,598 rows: `[label, kind, href, sub, weight]`); fetched the first time the palette opens. Never edit by hand.
- `events8.js` — the second bridge: nodes flagged `ar: [importance, theme, region, civ, title]` become archive records (19 of them, plus the Soviet famine and the Great Terror). Publishes `ARCHIVUS_DOCTRINE_LINKS` and two tours.

**To add an event:** append to `events4.js` following the schema, add its coordinate to the same file's pattern (`at: [lat, lon]`), reload.

**To add a battle to the War Room:** append an item to `items` in the right `wars-*.js` file (`id, n, s, e, at, k, w, d, f, u`); add `ar: [...]` if it should also appear in the Archive. **To move a front:** add a key `{ d: "YYYY-MM-DD", p: [[lat, lon], …] }` — the app resamples and interpolates. Validate with `node tools/validate-wars.js`.

**To add a doctrine:** append a node to `doctrines.js` with its family, coordinates, origin, `d`, `c`, `u` and `fr`; it appears on the Compass, Lineage, Atlas and Library at once. Placements are editorial conventions — keep the critics' case beside every doctrine. Validate with `node tools/validate-doctrines.js` (`--urls` lists every citation).

**After any change:** `node tools/build-mobile.js --zip` regenerates the search index and rebuilds the fourteen single-file `mobile/` builds and `exports/archivus-site.zip`. Sourcing standards and the research trail live in `research/`.

## Epistemics

Three registers, never blurred: **the record** (dated, sourced — Wikipedia orientation links, flagged for cross-checking in `research/sources.md`), **the claim** (the board's designations, the codex's transmissions — preserved verbatim, attributed), and **legend** (dashed, labeled — the Grail thread, the mythic strata). Contested dates carry visible uncertainty bands; interpolated chronologies say so. *The archive takes no position on daimons. It takes positions on dates.*
